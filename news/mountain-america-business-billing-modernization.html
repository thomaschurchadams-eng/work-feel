Mountain America Moves Business Billing Outside Its Core

November 7, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Business BankingBilling Operations

The credit union says a separate account-analysis platform has reduced manual work and branch billing inquiries. The announcement provides no counts or cost result.

Mountain America Credit Union has moved business-account analysis and billing from a legacy setup to a separate software-as-a-service platform, according to a November 6 announcement from provider SunTec. The provider release says the credit union has completed multiple billing cycles on the new system.

The implementation separates account-analysis calculations from the core system. SunTec says the platform can combine service use, transaction volume or value, balances, pricing rules, earnings credits, waivers and discounts into the charge shown on a business member's analysis statement. Mountain America already explains to members that its business statement package includes a separate analysis statement detailing deductions and earnings credits.

The result is directional, not quantified

SunTec reports fewer billing questions reaching Mountain America's branches and a substantial reduction in manual intervention. A Mountain America business-services executive says the implementation is simplifying operations and reducing manual effort. Those are attributable operating observations, but the public release does not provide inquiry counts, hours saved, error rates, implementation cost or a before-and-after measurement window.

That distinction matters for another credit union considering the same architecture. A completed billing cycle establishes that the system is operating; it does not establish the return on investment. A useful internal scorecard would keep at least four measures separate: adjustments after a statement closes, employee time spent investigating charges, member inquiries tied to analysis statements, and the percentage of configured discounts or earnings credits applied without exception.

A separate platform creates a reconciliation job

Decoupling account analysis from the core can make pricing rules easier to change, but it also creates boundaries that have to reconcile. Transaction data, collected balances, product eligibility and negotiated pricing must arrive for the correct member, account and period. The charge returned to the core must match the supporting statement, and an employee needs a traceable reason when it does not.

Credit unions should therefore ask which system is authoritative for each input, how late or corrected transactions are handled, who approves pricing-rule changes, and how a disputed charge is reproduced. The provider announcement does not disclose Mountain America's exception process, service levels, control testing or migration cost.

For finance and operations leaders, the takeaway is not simply that billing can sit outside the core. It is that flexibility needs a period-by-period reconciliation record, and qualitative improvement claims should be converted into local measures before the next investment decision.

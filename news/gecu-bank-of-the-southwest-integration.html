GECU Closes Bank of the Southwest Deal; Customer Services Stay Separate

November 5, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Bank AcquisitionOperationsCustomer Access

The deal closed November 1, but the two institutions expect to keep customer services separate until their systems and operations are combined, targeted for June 2027.

GECU Federal Credit Union has completed its acquisition of Bank of the Southwest, but the bank’s customers should keep using their existing accounts and service channels for now. The institutions legally combined November 1. In its November 2 announcement, GECU said the former bank will operate as BOSW by GECU while the two organizations integrate their systems.

GECU said the combined credit union has $4.9 billion in assets and serves more than 449,000 members. The deal expands its presence in New Mexico. The National Credit Union Administration, Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation and New Mexico Financial Institutions Division approved the transaction in August, according to GECU. That approval, the legal closing and the systems conversion are separate milestones.

The Bank of the Southwest customer FAQ gives June 2027 as the expected integration date, not a confirmed cutover. The November announcement does not give an exact conversion weekend or a detailed timetable for individual products.

What changes now, and what does not

Customers may see BOSW by GECU on signs, online and on replacement or renewed debit cards. That does not mean they should start using GECU’s separate online banking service or branches for their BOSW accounts. The customer FAQ says account numbers, online access, the contact center and how customers transact do not change immediately. It also says customers can continue to use their current checks and debit cards.

The distinction matters because a customer can see the acquiring institution’s name before its staff and systems can serve the acquired account. If a customer calls the wrong contact center after seeing the new name, the practical test is whether staff can route that customer to the right team without an unnecessary repeat call or conflicting instructions.

The conversion needs its own readiness decision

For credit-union integration teams, closing the deal answers who owns the accounts. It does not answer whether customers can move between the two service environments without losing access. A useful rehearsal would follow actual customer tasks: signing in after a password reset, checking a scheduled bill payment, replacing a debit card and resolving a transaction that is already in dispute. Each test needs a named owner for exceptions and a clear answer on which system holds the authoritative record before and after cutover. Those are operating implications, not controls GECU has reported implementing.

Our coverage of DFCU Financial’s proposed Gulfside Bank acquisition shows the earlier approval stage of a different transaction. Our reporting on the Sharetec outage illustrates a separate risk: when access is interrupted, branches and contact centers still need a dependable way to answer customers’ basic account questions. Nothing in GECU’s announcement indicates an outage.

The next useful update will be a dated, customer-facing conversion plan: which services move first, which may be temporarily unavailable and where customers go for help. Until then, June is a planning target, and BOSW’s existing service instructions remain the relevant guide.

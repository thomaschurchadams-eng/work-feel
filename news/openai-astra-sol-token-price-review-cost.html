OpenAI Cuts Astra Token Prices; Credit Unions Still Need to Price Review Time

November 18, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Operations

GPT-6.2 Astra has lower standard API rates. A cheaper token is only part of the cost of producing a financial document someone can accept.

OpenAI’s new GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol give credit-union operations teams another price comparison to make before choosing a model for document work. The company’s launch announcement, listed in its November 17 product releases, puts Astra’s standard API input and output token rates 20% below GPT-6 Astra’s.

The listed standard rates per million tokens are $8 for Astra input and $40 for output. Sol retains rates of $2 and $10 respectively. These are API rates, not the price of a ChatGPT subscription or a promise that an application vendor will reduce its own charges.

For a hypothetical job with 100,000 billed input tokens and 10,000 billed output tokens, that is $1.20 on Astra: $0.80 plus $0.40. The same token counts cost $0.30 on Sol. This is illustrative arithmetic, not an executed comparison; it excludes cached-input pricing, other services and human work. The models may use different quantities of tokens for the same assignment.

Compare the document that survives review

For a credit union preparing a management memo or financial workbook, the more useful denominator is an accepted deliverable. A draft that costs less to generate may take longer to reconcile, and an expensive draft may still be wrong. Neither result can be inferred from a rate card.

An operations team could give both models the same public annual report and ask for a short explanation of one financial movement, with source passages and arithmetic attached. Keep the task and acceptance criteria fixed, then record billed usage, correction time and whether the reviewer accepted the result. Use public or appropriately approved material rather than introducing member data into an evaluation by default.

OpenAI describes improvements in professional outputs and efficiency. Those are provider claims, not evidence of savings at a particular credit union. The price cut makes an evaluation less expensive at the listed rates; only the completed workflow can establish whether it makes the work cheaper.

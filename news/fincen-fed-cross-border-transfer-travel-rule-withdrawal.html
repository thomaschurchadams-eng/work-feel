FinCEN and Fed Withdraw $250 Cross-Border Transfer Proposal

November 9, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

PaymentsBSA/AML

The agencies withdrew a proposal to lower the threshold for certain cross-border transfer records from $3,000 to $250. Existing obligations remain in place.

FinCEN and the Federal Reserve have withdrawn a 2020 proposal that would have required financial institutions to collect and retain records on certain cross-border transfers starting at $250 instead of $3,000. FinCEN also proposed the same lower threshold for passing required information to the next institution in the payment chain. The withdrawal was announced November 6 and took effect November 9, according to the joint Federal Register notice.

For credit unions that handle international wires, this is a change to the regulatory watchlist, not to the wire system. The withdrawn proposal never took effect. Teams should not use the notice to change a threshold, remove an existing control, or assume a processor has updated its own rules.

What was withdrawn, and what stays

The proposal applied the lower threshold to transfers that begin or end outside the United States. It also sought to clarify how the rules apply to transactions involving convertible virtual currency and certain other digital assets. The agencies said they would take no further action on this proposal and cited its age, comments received and changes in the digital-asset market and legal framework. The withdrawal neither creates a new digital-asset obligation nor supplies a blanket exemption.

The existing federal rules still generally require financial institutions to retain records for covered transfers of $3,000 or more and to pass specified details along the payment chain where the Travel Rule applies. See 31 CFR 1020.410(a) and 31 CFR 1010.410(f) for the current text and its exceptions. The figure is not a general screening cutoff or an exemption from other BSA or sanctions obligations.

Close the proposed change, not the control

Payments and compliance teams can close the specific 2020 proposal in their change register while leaving existing requirements mapped to their owners. If a vendor roadmap or internal specification already reflects the proposed $250 threshold, confirm whether it is an unimplemented change, a test setting or a separate institution policy before removing anything. Record that decision so a future analyst can tell why the configuration differs from a vendor default.

For one representative cross-border wire already handled under the institution’s current policy, trace the originator details from the intake record to the outbound message and retained evidence. If a correspondent or service provider performs a step, identify who can retrieve the required information and how an exception gets back to the credit union. That test is independent of the withdrawn proposal.

The notice does not rule out a future rulemaking. For a separate example of why payment access and AML responsibilities must be mapped together, see our coverage of proposed Fed payment-account guardrails.

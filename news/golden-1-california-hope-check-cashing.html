Golden 1 Offers No-Fee Check Cashing to California HOPE Recipients

November 23, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Member Service

California seeded HOPE accounts with $3,000. A new partnership gives participants a place to cash their distribution checks and access one-on-one financial guidance.

Golden 1 Credit Union will cash distribution checks at no charge for young Californians enrolled in the state’s HOPE trust account program. The new partnership addresses a practical question for someone who has received a check: where can they turn it into money they can use without losing some of it to a check-cashing fee?

The California State Treasurer’s Office and Golden 1 announced the arrangement on November 23. Golden 1 also offers participants individual financial guidance and a checking account with no monthly maintenance fee or minimum balance requirement. The announcement does not say that opening a checking account is a condition of cashing the check, or that nonmembers can cash it without joining. Participants can ask Golden 1 about the requirements before making a trip.

A seed deposit, not a promised check amount

California launched HOPE in May for eligible young people who experienced extended foster care or lost a parent or guardian to COVID-19. Under the state program’s rules, each trust account starts with $3,000. The funds are invested and may grow. The initial deposit is not a statement of what any one participant’s check will be worth.

The state makes funds available for withdrawal between ages 18 and 27. A participant takes the full distribution at once, not in installments, and can decide how to spend or save it. The state handles program eligibility and distribution. Monday’s announcement concerns access to the proceeds, not a new enrollment period or an additional state payment.

What a branch can measure

For credit unions considering a similar partnership, opening an account and successfully cashing a check answer different questions. If a participant needs a second visit because the first branch could not explain what to bring, an account-opening count will not show it. A useful first measure would be how many participants obtain their funds on the first visit and at what cost. Demand for checking or financial guidance can be tracked separately. Neither partner reported take-up or participant outcomes in the announcement.

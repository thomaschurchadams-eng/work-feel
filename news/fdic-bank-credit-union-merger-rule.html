Credit Unions Challenge Special Merger Tests for Bank Acquisitions

November 24, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Board and Strategy

Credit-union advocates object to special FDIC treatment for bank sales to credit unions and to counting credit-union shares in an initial concentration screen.

Credit-union advocates are asking the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation to remove proposed merger provisions that would treat a bank sale to a credit union differently from a bank-to-bank deal. Their November 23 comment also challenges the proposal’s treatment of credit-union shares in the FDIC’s initial competitive-effects screen.

The America’s Credit Unions summary published November 24 describes positions submitted to the agency. They are recommendations in an open rulemaking record, not a final rule, an approval decision or evidence that a particular acquisition will close.

Two different review questions

The FDIC’s September 22 proposal would revise its Bank Merger Act process. Among other changes, it would account for credit unions and centrally booked deposits in the initial competitive analysis, create a deemed-approval route for some de minimis transactions and tailor filing requirements by size and risk.

A bank sale to a credit union already involves more than one regulator. The FDIC reviews the bank side, while the National Credit Union Administration reviews the acquiring credit union. The trade group asks the FDIC to use one standard for changes in products and services regardless of the buyer’s charter, and to explain how it will rely on NCUA findings without automatically moving a filing to a longer track.

Why the concentration screen matters

The proposal would include credit-union shares in an initial Herfindahl-Hirschman Index screen. That screen is a starting point for identifying market concentration; it is not by itself a finding that a deal harms competition. America’s Credit Unions argues that shares should be removed from the proposed screen and safe harbor, with credit-union competition considered case by case using evidence from the specific market.

For a credit union evaluating a bank acquisition, the useful preparation is not to assume either side will prevail. Keep a market file that separates the initial concentration calculation from the services members and customers actually use: deposits, branches, small-business products, digital access and pricing. Track the FDIC and NCUA records independently, and connect every integration commitment to the regulator that must evaluate it.

The comment deadline was November 23. The FDIC has not adopted a final version in the materials reviewed for this article, so teams should not rewrite merger procedures as though the proposal were already binding.

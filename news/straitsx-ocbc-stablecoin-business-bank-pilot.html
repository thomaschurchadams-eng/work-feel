StraitsX and OCBC Test Stablecoin Settlement Behind a Bank Account

November 24, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Payments and Deposits

A selected-flow B2B pilot puts the stablecoin leg behind a dedicated address while a participating business receives conventional funds in its existing bank account.

StraitsX and OCBC are piloting a cross-border business-payment flow in which a payer can use stablecoins while the receiving business continues to use its existing OCBC bank account.

The November 23 announcement names NTT DOCOMO ASIA as the first participant. It says the pilot initially covers selected cross-border B2B payment flows and remains subject to regulatory, compliance and operational requirements. That is a bounded pilot, not a general product launch.

The wallet address is an identification and reconciliation layer

Under the described model, StraitsX provides a dedicated wallet address for the receiving business. The address helps attribute an incoming stablecoin payment to that business, supporting payment identification, on-chain compliance, reconciliation and settlement. OCBC provides the recipient banking relationship and fiat off-ramp.

The result is meant to let a participating business receive funds in its existing account without directly managing the underlying stablecoin process. The Monetary Authority of Singapore’s BLOOM use-case list describes the same pattern: cross-border B2B payments over stablecoin rails, received as fiat in existing bank accounts.

What credit-union payment teams should ask

The architecture separates several records that must still agree: the payer’s stablecoin transfer, the wallet address, the business identity, compliance screening, conversion and the bank-account credit. A useful pilot record should bind those stages to one payment reference and show how exceptions are resolved.

Before drawing broader conclusions, a financial institution would need supported corridors and assets, cut-off behavior, fees and foreign-exchange treatment, settlement finality, returns and refunds, sanctions responsibilities, liquidity arrangements, statement detail and service-recovery procedures.

The announcement does not disclose a general availability date, transaction volume, pricing, supported corridor list or comparative performance results. It also does not identify a U.S. credit union as a participant. Its immediate value is a concrete operating model to examine—not evidence that stablecoin-backed business collections are ready for broad deployment.

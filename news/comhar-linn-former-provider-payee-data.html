Comhar Linn’s Notice Separates Former-Provider Data From Current Banking

November 22, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Vendor Management

The Irish credit union says historical payee information remained at a former provider. That is not a finding that its current banking system was compromised.

Comhar Linn INTO Credit Union published a November 20 notice about historical payee information retained by Wellington IT, a former provider to Education Credit Union. The Irish institution says the incident does not affect its current online banking or member funds.

Its member FAQ places current services with Progress Banking following Education’s transfer of engagements. It says affected members were emailed November 19, with postal notices planned for the week of November 23 where no email address is held. It warns about unexpected requests to change payment information or disclose security credentials. Those are the credit union’s statements; this newsroom has not independently examined the affected systems or the incident’s cause.

A former provider can remain in the data map

For U.S. credit-union vendor managers, the comparative lesson is a specific question about offboarding: after a service moves, what information remains with the former provider, and who can explain its purpose and retention? A finished migration is not, by itself, evidence that every old copy has been removed.

That question should not be turned into an allegation about this case. The FAQ does not establish a contractual breach, a failure to follow a destruction instruction or a particular attack method. Nor does an Irish notice establish a U.S. reporting obligation.

A credit union reviewing its own exit records could compare the agreed retention schedule with the provider’s confirmation and the member communications it would need if retained data later became involved in an incident. This is an operating consideration, not a claim that Comhar Linn lacked those records.

The distinction matters at the service desk as well. Staff need to explain which historical information is involved without telling members that a current account was compromised when the available evidence does not say that. Any further action should follow the institution’s current, verified instructions—not an assumed remedy copied from a different breach.

A 204,000-Loan Example Tests CDFI Transaction Reporting

November 24, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Risk and Compliance

A credit-union comment says loan-level CDFI reporting could turn one institution’s 204,000 consumer loans into separate records. The figure is a submitted example, not an agency finding.

A comment on proposed Community Development Financial Institutions Fund reporting changes says one large credit-union client originated more than 204,000 consumer loans in 2025. If each loan had to be extracted, formatted and validated as a separate record, the commenter argues, the operational burden would differ sharply from today’s aggregated consumer-loan reporting option.

The November 23 CDFI briefing from CU Strategic Planning summarizes three comment letters released publicly after their November 18 submission. The 204,000 figure is the firm’s client example. It is not a CDFI Fund estimate, a sector-wide loan count or evidence that the agency has decided to require record-by-record reporting.

Aggregate totals and loan records answer different questions

The CDFI Fund’s September 30 request for comment concerns the Annual Certification and Data Collection Report and the Abbreviated Transaction Level Report. The agency asks whether proposed collections are necessary and useful, whether burden estimates are accurate, and how technology could reduce respondent burden.

Existing CDFI Fund guidance describes a Consumer Loans/Investments object for regulated institutions. Aggregated reporting can show totals by approved target-market category. Transaction-level records can support different validation and analysis, but they also multiply extraction, formatting, quality-control and exception-handling work. The comment recommends keeping aggregate consumer-loan reporting available and finding a more targeted way to collect any additional detail.

What a compliance team can test now

Credit unions should avoid treating this comment as a final reporting instruction. Instead, inventory the fields available at origination, servicing and general-ledger layers; estimate how many records would need transformation; and identify where target-market classifications depend on data that is not stored consistently across systems.

A useful readiness test uses a representative sample rather than assuming every loan is equally easy to export. Check joint borrowers, refinances, purchased loans, charge-offs, reversals and loans that qualify under more than one target-market category. Record validation failures separately from successful rows so the institution can explain both data quality and processing capacity.

Comments are due November 30, 2026. The materials reviewed for this article do not establish a final reporting design, implementation date or agency decision on the aggregate option.

American Heritage Adds Installments After a Debit Purchase

November 16, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Lending and Collections

American Heritage lets eligible members finance a debit purchase after checkout. Its public materials show why offer terms need one source of truth.

American Heritage Credit Union has added a way for eligible members to turn a debit card purchase into an installment loan after the sale. In a November 12 announcement, the Philadelphia credit union said members can find qualifying purchases in digital banking, choose an offer and repay it from their American Heritage account. It named equipifi as the technology partner. The public materials do not show how many members are using the service or how those loans perform.

This is not an installment choice at the merchant’s checkout. The member first makes a debit purchase, and the credit union can then offer financing on the eligible transaction. That sequence leaves two records to support: the original purchase and any loan the member later accepts. A useful product test follows both when a purchase no longer goes as planned.

Start with the offer the member actually sees

The credit union’s current member FAQ directs users to the offers area of Mobile Teller or Online Teller. It says some transactions do not qualify, including cash withdrawals and loan payments, and that members can repay a plan early. The same FAQ describes eligible purchases starting at $100; a separate notice dated November 12 lists a $300–$3,000 range. The public sources do not explain the difference. Neither figure should be read as a promise of an offer for a particular member or purchase.

That discrepancy is a useful launch test for other lenders. Take an eligible and an ineligible transaction and compare the result in the app with what a contact-center employee would tell the member. Check the transaction window, exclusions and the terms presented before acceptance. If an offer depends on an account or purchase attribute, include a reason the employee can verify. A general product FAQ cannot resolve an individual offer.

Follow a return through the purchase and the loan

In a test environment, start with a debit purchase, accept a hypothetical installment offer, and then record a merchant refund. Ask which balance changes, when the member sees each change, and whether scheduled payments continue until another step is taken. Repeat with a partial refund and a card dispute. The public American Heritage sources cited here do not establish how its system handles those cases; these are test cases for credit unions evaluating a similar product, not reported outcomes.

Lending and card operations should agree on who answers each question and where the member goes if the two records disagree. Keep the receipt for the original card purchase distinct from the terms accepted for the loan. It is easy to demo the offer screen. A stronger test is whether a member and an employee can both explain what happens after an exception.

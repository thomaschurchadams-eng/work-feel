Fed Check Fees Rise in January; Processing Windows Shrink in May

November 16, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Payments and Deposits

Federal Reserve check fees rise in January 2027 and processing windows narrow in May. Credit unions can test the cost and timing separately.

The Federal Reserve has approved higher fees for several check-processing services starting January 1, 2027. The Reserve Banks also plan to shorten check processing on weekends and close during holiday windows beginning in May. The dates affect different work: January is a budget change; May is a scheduling and reconciliation change.

The Federal Reserve’s November 12 announcement estimates that prices for its established payment services will rise 1.4% on average. The approved fee notice estimates a 5.4% average increase for Check Services customers. Neither estimate tells a credit union what it will pay. That depends on how it sends and receives checks, which services it buys, and whether it clears through the Reserve Banks directly or through a correspondent.

Price the checks your credit union actually processes

One way to estimate the effect is to rerun a recent month’s invoice at the 2027 rates, keeping volumes fixed. For instance, the notice lists FedReceipt forward and return item fees rising from $0.0005 to $0.0015 per item. For 100,000 items charged at that rate, that line alone would go from $50 to $150, before any other fees or contract terms. This is an illustration from the notice, not a forecast for any credit union.

The average also obscures what a credit union buys. In the notice, fees for several premium services rise by roughly 20%, while the Presentment Point service family is being retired. Start with the actual line items and identify features not used. If your processor clears on your behalf, ask which changes it will pass through and when; the Federal Reserve’s fee schedule does not establish the price in your processor contract.

Test what happens to a weekend deposit

Beginning in May, the Reserve Banks’ general check processing schedule moves from a Sunday 2 p.m. Eastern opening to Sunday 7 p.m., and from a Saturday 10 p.m. close to Saturday 4 a.m. Holiday windows vary. The notice says more detailed customer communications are forthcoming. The change concerns how institutions process through the Reserve Banks; it does not prevent consumers or businesses from writing or receiving checks.

Trace one Friday deposit and one Sunday mobile deposit from the member’s screen through the check processor and the institution’s reconciliation. Determine when each is accepted, sent to the clearing provider and posted to the ledger. The Fed’s operating hours, the credit union’s cutoff and a member’s funds-availability date are different clocks. Review actual provider schedules and applicable requirements before changing a disclosure or service promise.

For payments and treasury leaders, the first useful deliverables are an invoice comparison for January and a dated weekend and holiday test for May. If your provider absorbs a fee change or already stops collecting checks before the shorter windows, record that too. The amount of work is determined by your clearing arrangement, not the national percentage.

Higher-Priced Mortgage Appraisal Exemption Rises to $35,500

November 26, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Lending and Collections

The 2027 small-loan exemption threshold rises by $1,300, but the higher-priced mortgage appraisal framework remains in place.

The federal banking agencies have raised a narrow mortgage-compliance threshold for 2027. Higher-priced mortgage loans of $35,500 or less can qualify for the small-loan exemption from the special appraisal requirements, up from $34,200 in 2026. The change takes effect January 1.

For credit unions, the operational task is precise: update the threshold in origination and quality-control systems, preserve the evidence behind the change, and avoid turning a $1,300 adjustment into a broader claim that an appraisal is unnecessary.

The rule changes one amount, not the HPML framework

The Federal Reserve, Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and Office of the Comptroller of the Currency said the new threshold reflects a 3.9% annual increase in the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers as of June 1, 2026.

The underlying higher-priced mortgage appraisal rule generally requires a written appraisal based on a physical visit to the home’s interior. Loans at or below the annually adjusted amount are exempt from those special requirements. The agencies’ announcement does not change the tests used to determine whether a mortgage is higher priced, and it does not describe a general exemption from valuation, ability-to-repay, fair-lending, investor or safety-and-soundness requirements.

A small threshold can create a large control problem

The dollar change is modest, but a stale value can create inconsistent treatment across channels. Credit unions should identify every place the threshold is encoded or referenced: loan-origination rules, product configuration, compliance testing, exception queues, procedures, staff guides, vendor interfaces and post-closing review.

That inventory matters because the decision may be distributed. One system can determine the loan amount, another can classify the HPML, and a third can order or waive the appraisal. If only one component receives the 2027 value, the same application could produce different outcomes depending on channel or workflow.

A controlled implementation should bind the new amount to an effective date, retain the previous value for loans evaluated under the earlier period, and prevent a future-dated configuration from changing 2026 files. Tests should cover $35,499, exactly $35,500 and $35,501, along with the institution’s normal rounding and amendment scenarios.

Vendor confirmation should be evidence, not assumption

A vendor statement that its platform is “updated” is not the same as proof that the credit union’s configuration, integrations and downstream review all use the right value. Mortgage and compliance leaders can request the release identifier, effective-date behavior and test evidence, then run a small institution-owned sample through the complete path.

Any manual override should record who made it, why, which threshold applied and what independent review followed. That is especially important around year-end, when an application can be created under one calendar year and reach a later decision stage in another.

Use the annual update to test ownership

The adjustment is predictable and bounded, which makes it a useful control test. A named owner should confirm the source, a change owner should update the governed configuration, and a separate reviewer should verify the boundary cases and stored evidence. The final record should show the agency release, the effective date, the deployed rule version and the results of the institution’s own test cases.

The goal is not more process for a small dollar change. It is a repeatable way to keep annual thresholds synchronized across people, systems and vendors—without weakening the appraisal protections that still apply outside the exemption.

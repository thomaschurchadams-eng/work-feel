Allegacy Picks One AI Platform for Vendor Risk and Compliance

November 10, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Vendor RiskCompliance

The North Carolina credit union plans to move vendor risk, enterprise risk and compliance into one system. The announcement gives no rollout date or results.

Allegacy Financial has selected Kobalt Labs to replace its legacy risk and compliance software, putting vendor reviews, enterprise risk and regulatory tracking in the same planned system. The North Carolina credit union also intends to move business continuity and contract management onto the platform, according to the vendor’s November 9 announcement, which includes comments from Allegacy’s chief legal officer.

The decision goes beyond automating one vendor questionnaire. It puts several workflows that can rely on the same underlying documents into a common system. A vendor report, for example, can be relevant to a third-party review and to a business-continuity plan, but a finding in one workflow does not necessarily resolve the other. That distinction matters when staff start relying on AI-generated summaries or findings across departments.

What moves, and what remains unverified

Kobalt says its agents review documents, identify possible policy gaps and track regulatory changes, with source citations and audit trails. The announced workflow still puts decisions and remediation with staff. The release does not specify a go-live date, which functions will move first, or how Allegacy will validate results after cutover. It also does not establish that reviews have already become faster or that any gaps have been resolved.

The breadth is the notable change from ORNL Federal Credit Union’s earlier Kobalt selection, which centered on vendor risk reviews. The same provider can be used for very different scopes. Due diligence on one workflow should not be assumed to cover policy monitoring, continuity or contract records.

Test a decision across two teams

Before moving several risk workflows, credit unions can run one vendor control exception through the old and proposed systems. For example, take a missing recovery-test report that affects both a vendor review and the continuity team’s assessment. Check that the teams see the same source and version, that the system distinguishes missing evidence from satisfactory evidence, and that resolving a task in one workflow does not silently approve the other.

Ask whether an auditor can reconstruct the decision from the source, the finding and the named approver without asking the model to regenerate it. That is a migration acceptance test, not a claim about Allegacy’s rollout.

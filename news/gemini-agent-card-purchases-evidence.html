Gemini Adds Card Purchases, but a Spending Cap Is Not a Dispute Record

November 18, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Payments and Deposits

Google says its task assistant can shop with supported cards. Issuers still need a way to connect a questioned purchase to the member’s instruction.

Google’s task assistant is moving beyond finding a product to helping buy it. In its November 17 Gemini update, the company describes Google Pay checkout using the Agent Payments Protocol, or AP2. For credit-union card teams, the useful question is what evidence connects a completed purchase to what the member asked the assistant to do.

Google says the service uses digital tokens on supported cards through work with Mastercard and Visa; American Express is listed as coming later. It describes an upfront spending cap and confirmation or pre-approval before a purchase. Receipts go to Google Wallet. Advanced features are starting in the United States, and the announcement does not establish that every credit union’s card is supported.

Those are provider-described controls, not results from a CUAI transaction test. Google acknowledges that tasks can fail or require the user to complete a final step. A successfully funded or authorized transaction does not, by itself, explain the entire conversation that preceded it.

The amount alone may not explain the complaint

Consider a hypothetical member who permits an assistant to buy a particular item within a budget, then questions the merchant or version ordered. The amount could be below the cap while the member’s concern remains unresolved. An issuer investigating that question needs to distinguish the purchase record from any available record of the instruction, confirmation and actual item.

A practical first inquiry is what the member, merchant, wallet and issuer can each retrieve without assuming that they all see the same information. Ask where the final confirmation appears, which receipt identifies the item and how the transaction can be matched to it. Do not presume that an agent’s conversation is exposed to the issuer.

This is an evidence question for payments and member-service teams, not a conclusion about legal liability or a reason to bypass existing dispute procedures. A cap can constrain spending; it cannot answer every question about whether the requested purchase was the one delivered.

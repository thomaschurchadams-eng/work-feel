NCUA Will Repeal Its Fair-Lending Rule Without Changing Federal Protections

November 16, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Risk and Compliance

The NCUA’s fair-lending rule repeal takes effect December 16. Federal credit unions still have the same obligations under federal law.

The National Credit Union Administration will remove its separate fair-lending regulation for federal credit unions on December 16. The final rule, published November 16, rescinds 12 CFR 701.31. It does not change the lending, appraisal or advertising obligations that federal credit unions have under the Fair Housing Act or the Equal Credit Opportunity Act.

For a compliance team, the distinction is between updating a citation and retiring the work that citation was attached to. A policy or checklist that points to Part 701 should be reviewed to determine what still needs to happen and which authority governs it. The repeal, on its own, is not a reason to drop a review or tell a lending team that a practice is newly permitted.

What comes off the books

The NCUA first issued the regulation in 1972 to summarize requirements for federal credit unions. Its last substantive revision was in 2001. The agency said the text no longer reflected later developments in fair-housing law and repeated requirements administered by other agencies. It adopted the proposal without change. The provision being removed did not apply to federally insured, state-chartered credit unions; that distinction is not an exemption from other applicable fair-lending law.

Federal credit unions remain subject to the Fair Housing Act and the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, including applicable HUD rules and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau’s Regulation B. The final rule specifically says that applicable fair-housing notices remain required. It also addresses appraisal concerns: removing the NCUA’s summary does not alter obligations under the underlying laws. The November 13 announcement covered four final rules. The December 16 date here is for the nondiscrimination repeal, not a single effective date for that whole group.

Start with the citation, not the control

Federal credit union compliance staff can start with policies and procedures that cite 701.31 directly. Look beyond the policy manual: copies of the citation may also be in appraisal review instructions, mortgage ad approvals, branch notice checklists or vendor work instructions. For each reference, name the owner, identify the current authority with compliance or counsel, and record where the team keeps evidence that the work was done. If a third party prepares advertisements or supports appraisals, check the instructions it actually uses rather than assuming a policy update reaches it.

The NCUA says credit unions may use their usual methods and timeframes to remove obsolete references. It did not set a separate accelerated documentation project. A useful check for the next policy review is to take one recurring step, such as approving a mortgage advertisement, and ask the person who performs it to show the applicable rule, the instructions and a completed review. A changed citation without a change in the work is the expected result of this repeal; any proposed change to the work needs its own basis.

Fed Survey Finds Weekend Payment Plans Outpace Added Liquidity Staffing

November 22, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Finance and Treasury

Thirty of 91 banks offer or plan more weekend tools; three reported more liquidity staff working weekends. These are not credit-union adoption rates.

More banks reported expanding or planning weekend payment and liquidity tools than reported putting additional liquidity staff on weekend duty, according to the Federal Reserve’s September Senior Financial Officer Survey, released November 20.

The survey’s expanded-hours results show 30 of 91 responding banks—33%—offering or planning more payment and liquidity-management options on weekends and holidays. Three, or 3.3%, reported more liquidity operations staff working then; all three were foreign banking organizations. Twenty-six, or 28.6%, reported increased or planned technology spending to anticipate wider liquidity operations.

The questions allowed multiple responses. The figures therefore describe different reported actions, not mutually exclusive groups or a measured staffing deficit. Plans and services already offered are combined in the first measure.

What the sample does—and does not—show

The Fed’s release identifies a bank survey, not a credit-union survey. Replies were collected in September. The sample included 56 domestic banks and 35 foreign banking organizations, together holding about three-quarters of banking-system reserve balances at the time.

Credit-union treasury leaders should not read 33% as an adoption rate for their peers, or 3.3% as the staffing level they need. The results do provide a reason to compare service availability with the authority available when an exception occurs.

For example, a hypothetical weekend payment disruption could require someone to assess balances, contact a funding counterparty and approve an action. A roster is useful only if those responsibilities match the services actually offered. This is an operating inference, not a causal finding from the survey.

The immediate decision is to test that handoff against the credit union’s own arrangements. New hours, planned technology investment and additional staff are separate evidence. None alone proves the institution can resolve a liquidity exception when the normal weekday team is unavailable.

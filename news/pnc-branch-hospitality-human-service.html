PNC Puts Hospitality Alongside Its Branch Expansion

November 21, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Member Service

PNC is expanding its branch network while emphasizing hospitality. The human-service approach is established; an AI-era payoff remains an expectation.

PNC is pairing a substantial branch-expansion plan with a service approach borrowed from hospitality. Banking Dive reported on November 19 that the bank is emphasizing hospitality experience, local hiring and personal interaction as it prepares for a banking market increasingly influenced by AI-generated advice.

The physical expansion is concrete but prospective. In its November 6 announcement, PNC said its plan now covers more than 370 new branches by 2031, with 60 openings a year starting in 2027. That is a buildout plan, not a count of branches already open.

The service idea is not a new AI launch

PNC had already described its hospitality approach in an October 31, 2025 explanation. It discussed formal training, welcoming customers at the door, employees with hospitality backgrounds and feedback after a visit. The current reporting places that established approach in an AI-era competitive argument; it does not make the program newly launched.

For credit union member-service leaders, the useful comparison is between the roles of a digital answer and an in-person interaction. A member may arrive with information already gathered elsewhere but still need help explaining a situation, resolving an exception or understanding the next step. The value of a branch cannot be established simply by counting how many routine questions a chatbot can answer.

That does not prove a hospitality investment will generate a particular return. The expectation that personal service becomes more valuable alongside AI is a strategic judgment, not measured evidence of higher retention, additional deposits or lower service cost. PNC's branch announcement also does not establish that another institution should match its building or hiring plan.

A credit union can make the comparison more concrete using its own service evidence: what people seek when they arrive, which requests staff can finish and where the handoff breaks. A warm welcome and an accurate resolution are complementary, not interchangeable, parts of that experience.

The practical lesson is narrower than a prediction that branches win or lose against AI. Keep the investment claim, the service design and the observed member result separate. PNC offers a visible example of a bank investing in both place and interaction; the business outcome still needs to be demonstrated.

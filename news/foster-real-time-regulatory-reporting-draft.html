Foster Draft Would Test Real-Time Reporting for Financial Regulators

November 6, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Regulatory ReportingOpen-Source Software

A congressional discussion draft calls for a Federal Reserve software test. For credit unions, the question is whether faster reporting could replace work they already do.

Rep. Bill Foster released a discussion draft on November 5 that would have the Federal Reserve test open-source software for faster financial reporting. The Illinois Democrat’s announcement says the Fed would work with other financial regulators to develop, test and publish demonstration software. The proposal does not change what credit unions file today.

Foster’s office says the proposed study would examine whether software could give regulators timelier information while simplifying reporting and lowering compliance costs. The office also points to faster detection of financial stress, access to liquidity and better preparation for resolving troubled institutions. Those are objectives for the study, not demonstrated benefits or an announced rollout.

A faster report is only useful if it replaces work

Many fields in a regulatory submission come from systems with different closing times and definitions. For example, a payment may be authorized in one system before it settles in another. A faster feed could send that field more often without making it more useful. A credible test would show when the value was observed, where it came from and how a correction reaches the regulator.

The Defense Credit Union Council said it was reserving judgment while it reviewed the proposal and sought member input. It urged Foster to deliver measurable benefits for smaller credit unions without adding unnecessary burden. A useful benchmark would compare the hours and vendor work required to produce and reconcile one report today with the work needed to run the demonstration. If institutions still submit the old report and also maintain the new feed, faster data alone would not show lower compliance cost.

A software demonstration, not a new filing calendar

The sponsor’s announcement describes public release of the demonstration software, not public release of institution records. A practical design would need to distinguish the code other teams can inspect from the financial data they are allowed to see. It would also need to show how a small institution could participate without buying a new reporting system. Those are questions for evaluating a future test, not requirements attributed to the draft.

This is separate from NCUA’s proposed spending on its own supervisory technology. It also differs from the reported control gaps in bank AI adoption: the question here is whether a regulator can use faster, more reliable data without shifting more work to the institutions producing it.

Reporting and compliance teams do not need to change their filing calendars. If the draft advances, the next useful details will be who can participate, which data the demonstration would use, and how it would measure savings after accounting for reconciliation, vendor changes and any parallel filing.

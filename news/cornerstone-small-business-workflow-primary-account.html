Small-Business Research Separates the Primary Account From the Daily Workflow

November 19, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Vendor Management

A Fiserv-commissioned study contrasts who holds the relationship with where work happens. Stated consolidation intent is not a deposit forecast.

Small businesses can keep their primary financial relationship with a bank or credit union while doing substantial daily work elsewhere, according to Cornerstone Advisors research announced November 17. The study was commissioned by Fiserv, which supplies financial technology to banks and credit unions.

The announcement says about one in ten surveyed businesses consider a fintech their primary provider, while roughly four in ten have a fintech account. Those are different measures: holding the principal account does not mean hosting every payment, invoice or cash-flow workflow.

Research-announcement brief: Cornerstone reports surveying more than 1,000 US business owners and executives at companies with annual revenue from $100,000 to $100 million. These are not results for a separately identified credit-union member sample.

Nearly 75% said they would consolidate more financial activity with their primary bank or credit union if it matched the capabilities of their most-valued fintech. That is conditional stated intent, not observed switching or a forecast of deposits a credit union will win.

Start with where the work happens

For business-service and vendor-management leaders, the practical question is which missing workflow matters enough to justify an integration or a product change. The research announcement does not establish that buying a particular platform will deepen a relationship.

Consider a hypothetical business member who deposits receipts at the credit union but manages invoices in accounting software and payroll through another provider. A checking-account balance alone would not reveal whether the owner is struggling with reconciliation, payment timing or something unrelated. A conversation about the actual workflow can test the problem before procurement starts.

The public release does not provide a CU-specific subgroup, response rate, weighting or fieldwork dates; this brief did not retrieve the full report. Nor does it demonstrate the outcome of a CU software purchase.

A useful next decision is narrower than replacing the business account: establish what members already use, what they actually want connected and which data they have authorized the institution to receive. Keep that evidence separate from survey interest and from a supplier's proposed solution.

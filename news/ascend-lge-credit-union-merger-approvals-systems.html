Ascend and LGE Win Merger Approvals, but Systems Will Stay Separate Into 2028

November 13, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Board and Strategy

The credit unions announced member and regulatory approvals. Their merger is set for January 1, while systems integration is expected in early 2028.

Ascend Federal Credit Union and LGE Community Credit Union said November 10 that they had received both regulatory approval and approval from LGE members. The two institutions plan to combine legally on January 1, 2027. The date to watch for changes in how members bank is later: the institutions expect to integrate their operations in early 2028.

The combined institution will keep Ascend’s name and federal charter. LGE will continue as an Ascend division under its existing name until core conversion is complete. The two credit unions say the resulting organization will have more than $7 billion in assets, more than 390,000 members and more than 40 branches across Tennessee and Georgia. Those figures describe the expected combined institution; they do not mean members can use all those branches or systems interchangeably today.

What changes in January, and what does not

Ascend’s member FAQ tells existing Ascend members no action is required now. It says LGE will continue using its current systems during the transition. The FAQ also says fixed-rate loans and certificates will retain their rates through their existing terms, while other rates may change in the ordinary course. The announcement does not give a cutover weekend or publish a single cross-network service schedule.

The approval milestone distinguishes this deal from mergers still awaiting a regulator or member vote. The NCUA’s public merger listing recorded the LGE member vote date as November 9. The institutions’ announcement reports the result and regulatory approval; their early-2028 integration estimate is still a plan, not evidence that systems are ready.

Run service on two calendars

For a merger team, the legal date and the systems date answer different questions. The first tells staff which institution is accountable. The second determines when a member can use a different channel without being redirected. A joint name on a notice does not tell a branch employee whether they can see an LGE transaction, replace a card, or help with a locked digital account.

During the transition, a single service reference can list which channels can handle each request, who owns an escalation and what staff should tell a member who arrives at the wrong branch or calls the wrong number. Mark the dates and assumptions explicitly, and update the reference when access actually changes. That is more useful than promising an integrated experience a year ahead of the estimated conversion.

Ascend and LGE have cleared the announced approval steps. The next evidence to watch is the member-facing conversion plan: which services will change, when they change and how the institutions will handle exceptions while the two systems are still separate.

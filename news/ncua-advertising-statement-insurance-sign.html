The NCUA Drops the Ad Statement, Not the Insurance Sign

November 17, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Risk and Compliance

A final rule removes the official wording requirement for credit-union ads on December 16. The sign at branches and digital transaction points stays.

Federally insured credit unions will no longer have to put the official NCUA advertising statement in their ads under a final rule published November 16. The rule removes 12 CFR 740.5 and takes effect December 16, 2026. It leaves the separate requirement to display the official insurance sign in place.

The dates matter for campaigns already scheduled to run. The NCUA’s November 13 announcement summarizes the change, but the published rule gives December 16 as the effective date. That date belongs in a creative-approval record; it need not trigger a wholesale redesign. Credit unions may continue using accurate insured-status wording voluntarily after the change.

One message has two different jobs

An ad is separate from a place where a member is opening or funding an account. The final rule retains the official sign requirement at stations or windows where insured funds are ordinarily received and on internet pages where the credit union accepts deposits or opens accounts. The Board also describes how its existing approach applies to mobile banking and membership applications. The final rule does not make those signs optional.

The general rule against inaccurate or deceptive advertising also stays. A campaign that uses insurance language alongside a noninsured product still needs to distinguish the two. Removing the prescribed statement does not make an inaccurate claim acceptable, and the change is not an expansion of which products are insured.

Review the campaign and the account-opening page separately

Start with a scheduled campaign that links to a deposit or membership application. Marketing can identify any wording it plans to change and which versions will remain in rotation after December 16. Compliance and the digital team can open the destination independently, including the mobile path, and confirm that a change to shared artwork or templates would not also remove the sign where it is required. Keep those two decisions separate in the approval record.

That small review catches a practical risk: a team could remove a single shared asset to simplify an ad and accidentally remove it from an account-opening screen. The new rule gives marketing more flexibility, but the transaction point still needs to tell members what is insured.

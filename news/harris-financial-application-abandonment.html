Account-Opening Abandonment Often Comes at Funding and Identity Checks

November 18, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Marketing and Growth

A Harris Poll survey points to friction before approval. Its figures describe people, not an institution’s application conversion rate.

When applicants leave an online financial application, a faster approval engine may not address the step that stopped them. A new Cotribute-sponsored Harris Poll study points to funding and identity checks as common reported exit points, giving credit-union growth teams a reason to examine the journey before spending more to attract applicants.

The public findings say 61% of U.S. adults have abandoned an online financial-product application. Among people who had abandoned one, 29% applied for the same type of product elsewhere within 30 days, while 10% tried again at the original institution. Those are shares of people answering a survey—not the proportion of applications lost by a particular bank or credit union.

In the November 17 release, 46% of abandoners reported leaving when asked to fund an account and 42% at identity-document upload. These answers need not be mutually exclusive. They do not establish that removing either control would improve completed, legitimate account openings.

Find the missing step, not just the missing applicant

A useful local comparison separates a person who starts an application from a completed application, an approved account and an account that receives usable funds. If the dashboard stops at approval, a marketing team may count an apparent win that never becomes an active relationship.

For example, a hypothetical applicant could reach approval but abandon an external-account transfer, then restart on another device. One person can produce several sessions and more than one incomplete record. Reconcile those records before treating the survey as a conversion benchmark or comparing acquisition channels.

The published methodology describes two online surveys, each involving more than 2,000 U.S. adults, conducted in October and November 2026. Cotribute sells account-opening technology and sponsored the research. Self-reported national experience is a useful prompt for investigation, not an audit of a credit union’s funnel or evidence that the sponsor’s product fixes it.

Credit Unions Seek Clear Boundaries in Treasury’s Institutional-Investor Rule

November 24, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Risk and ComplianceLending and Collections

A credit-union comment asks Treasury to keep foreclosure, loss-mitigation and CUSO property transfers from being mistaken for prohibited investment purchases.

Credit-union advocates want Treasury to clarify that acquiring or transferring a home through foreclosure and loss mitigation does not become a prohibited investment purchase simply because a lender, subsidiary or credit union service organization touches the property.

The November 24 summary from America’s Credit Unions describes comments on Treasury’s proposed institutional-investor rule. The group supports the proposal’s measured treatment of defaulted loans and asks for three targeted clarifications. These are recommendations from a commenter, not provisions Treasury has adopted.

A 350-home threshold does not answer every foreclosure question

Treasury’s October 22 proposal implements Section 1001 of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act. Beginning January 7, 2027, a “large institutional investor” generally could not purchase a single-family home unless an exception applies. The statutory definition includes additional conditions, but one threshold is direct or indirect investment control over at least 350 single-family homes.

The proposal addresses debts previously contracted, loss mitigation and transfers between large institutional investors. America’s Credit Unions asks Treasury to confirm that credit unions can qualify under the same framework as similarly situated lenders; that foreclosure and loss-mitigation exceptions apply whether or not the foreclosing lender is itself a large institutional investor; and that a transfer to a subsidiary or CUSO does not automatically disqualify the transfer or a later sale from an applicable exception.

Map property custody before the rule is final

A credit union should separate ordinary mortgage origination from the much narrower path that begins with a defaulted loan. Document which entity takes title, why it does so, how the property is held, who authorizes repairs or disposition, and whether a subsidiary or CUSO becomes part of the chain.

That map should also identify direct and indirect control. A property may move between legal entities without changing the business purpose, but the rule’s application will depend on final definitions and exceptions—not merely on an internal label such as “foreclosure inventory.”

The comment deadline has passed. The reviewed materials do not establish a final Treasury rule, a final interpretation of CUSO transfers or a conclusion that any particular credit union meets the large-institutional-investor definition.

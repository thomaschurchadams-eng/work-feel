Google Opens Gemini 4 Carbon to Paying Users, With a Separate Route for Cyber Defenders

November 13, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

IT, Data and Cybersecurity

Google widened paid access to Gemini 4 Carbon. Credit unions should distinguish the restricted cyber program and test costs at the December rate.

Google widened access to Gemini 4 Carbon on November 10, making its latest model available to paying developers and enterprises. The change gives credit union technology teams a way to compare Carbon with a less expensive model on approved internal work. It does not put every team into Google’s separate cyber-defender program or show that Carbon performs better in a credit union’s environment.

Two paths, different entitlements

Google says paying developers can use Carbon via the Gemini API in AI Studio, and enterprises can access it through Gemini for business. The DeepMind model card lists the channels separately and describes Fairwind as access for verified cybersecurity defenders. Google says eligible defenders can apply on a rolling basis. A paid subscription should not be treated as confirmation that a security team has the restricted access it wants.

Google reports results in coding and security evaluations. Those tasks do not test a particular credit union’s systems, data-handling rules or review procedures. An initial comparison can use a synthetic incident ticket, a public advisory or a lab-only code sample. Give Carbon and the existing model the same material and score whether each identifies the right issue, cites the supporting evidence and leaves a reviewer with a safe next step.

Budget for the rate after November 30

Google’s introductory public-model rate is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. After November 30, Google says those rates rise to $4 and $20, respectively. For an illustrative workload of 5 million input and 1 million output tokens, those two line items would move from $20 to $40 before any other charges or discounts. That is arithmetic on Google’s stated rates, not a price quote or a measured test.

Run the comparison on cost per accepted result, including retries and time spent checking the answer. Keep member data out of the trial until the credit union has approved the specific service, terms and data flow. The practical decision is which jobs, if any, improve enough to justify paying the December rate.

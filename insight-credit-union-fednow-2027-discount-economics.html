FedNow’s 2027 Credits Come With Volume Tests and a Fee Cap

November 12, 2026

By CreditUnionAI News Staff

Finance and Treasury

A FedNow incentive is worth the amount a credit union can qualify for and retain. The program’s fee cap, sender thresholds and service requirements belong in the business case before a headline discount does.

Credit unions evaluating FedNow for 2027 have a new financial incentive to consider. Federal Reserve Financial Services will offer statement credits for qualifying send and receive capabilities beginning January 1. The program announcement, published September 15, also says the existing origination discount on the first 2,500 monthly items will end.

The business case therefore needs a fresh comparison. An institution may receive a credit while losing an existing discount, adding supporting services and incurring implementation costs. The practical question for a CFO is how much of the incentive the credit union can retain after meeting its obligations.

Start with the eligible amount

The 2027 fee schedule lists the following maximum credits. The actual statement credit cannot exceed the customer’s total fees paid in the preceding 12 months. Both new and existing FedNow participants can qualify.

Institution’s FedACH statusReceive maximumSend maximum
FedNow enabled; no higher FedACH tier$5,000$20,000
Eligible for FedACH Receipt Discount Program$10,000$40,000
Participating in FedACH Receipt Discount Program$20,000$80,000

That distinction between eligibility and participation is consequential. A credit union should establish its applicable tier and the effect of the fee cap with its FRFS relationship manager before treating a maximum amount as a budget assumption. The schedule alone does not establish a particular institution’s entitlement.

FRFS has also published the continuing transaction prices: 4.5 cents for a customer credit transfer origination, with the general $25 monthly participation fee per routing number discounted to zero in 2027. These prices help identify the Federal Reserve component of operating cost. They do not supply a processor’s charges, integration expense, staffing needs or fraud losses.

The sender credit has a performance condition

Under the program terms, the basic send tier requires at least 100 transactions a month. The higher tiers require at least 2,500. Existing senders already at the applicable threshold must increase average monthly send volume by at least 50% over the prior-year baseline.

Institutions must opt in. Half the credit is applied then and half after qualification. The volume test must be met in six qualifying months within the calendar year or six months after opt-in, whichever period is longer; the months need not be consecutive. Completed customer credit transfer originations count. Failure to meet the test can reverse the initial credit.

Consider an illustrative credit union qualifying for the full $20,000 basic send incentive after application of the fee cap. The payment sequence would be $10,000 at opt-in and $10,000 after qualification. If adoption falls short, the first $10,000 may have to be reversed. That makes a forecast of actual completed payments more useful than a forecast of registrations or enrolled accounts.

For internal planning, show the project with the full eligible incentive and again with no retained incentive. This is an economic sensitivity analysis, not a recommendation about accounting recognition. It makes the dependence on payment adoption visible to the committee approving the investment.

Receiving also carries continuing obligations

The receive credit requires full participation in the Exception Resolution Service, a FedPayments Insights subscription, and either FedDetect Anomaly Notification Premier or an integrated pre-transaction fraud service. Required services must remain active for six months. A failure to maintain them can reverse the credit.

Those services may be useful in their own right. Finance and payments leaders should still establish the incremental cost of the chosen combination and whether it fits existing fraud and reconciliation work. A service already in use has a different economic effect from one purchased solely to qualify.

The result should be a costed operating decision: an eligible credit amount, a realistic usage forecast, the expenses that continue after the credit, and an owner for monitoring qualification. FRFS’s program can improve the economics of sending or receiving instant payments. A credible credit union business case shows how the service will remain useful once the incentive has been earned.

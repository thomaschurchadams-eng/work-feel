Dollar General’s price lawsuit expands to 30 states

Lead source: November 20, 2026

Amended complaint filed: November 20, 2026

Pending lawsuit. No refund claim process announced.

Ten more states joined a federal lawsuit over alleged gaps between advertised prices and what shoppers paid at checkout. Here’s how to document a mismatch.

The Federal Trade Commission filed an amended complaint on November 20, adding Delaware, Georgia, Kentucky, Maine, Minnesota, New Mexico, Oregon, Rhode Island, South Carolina and South Dakota. The FTC and the original 20 states filed the case on October 26 in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Tennessee.

The complaint alleges that Dollar General repeatedly charged more at checkout than prices displayed on shelves, tags or in advertisements. Oregon’s attorney general says the suit asks the court to order pricing changes, penalties and money back for overcharged shoppers. Those are requests, not a court finding or a refund program. The case is pending, and the November 20 announcement did not open a claim process or establish a refund deadline.

In its October 26 response to the original lawsuit, Dollar General said discrepancies can happen despite its pricing procedures, that store teams are expected to address them, and that it disputes how the FTC calculated potential refunds and will defend the case.

What you can do

If a price looks wrong, save the itemized receipt and, if you can, photograph the shelf tag with the product size and any sale terms visible. Ask the store to review the mismatch. If it’s unresolved, your state consumer protection office can take a complaint. Filing a complaint is not a refund claim.
